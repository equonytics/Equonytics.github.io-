Specific examples of our work

Four analyses that led to specific strategic changes

Each of these started as a plain question: where's the money going? Why did this account leave? The resulting analyses yielded decisions the client actually made. Click a card to see the analysis and the change it drove. These are illustrative patterns we commonly see, not named client data.

On-time Delivery & Margins

Finding the service level that actually pays for itself

A regression on 21,316 orders that told the client where to stop chasing on-time delivery and where to reconfigure their focus.

View analysis
On-time delivery vs. gross margin lifthover to inspect
Every 1-point improvement was associated with a 0.4-point increase in gross margin, but only after service crossed 92%.
Change made: the client stopped funding uniform service-level targets network-wide and redirected the budget to push underperforming lanes past 92%, where the margin actually shows up.

Warehouse Slotting

Regrouping SKUs around how warehouse workers actually pick

Clustering co-picked items exposed a warehouse layout fighting its own pickers. This analysis resulted in a redesign of their slotting & picking practices.

View analysis
SKU clustering by pick frequency & weighthover for details
We found three natural clusters of items frequently picked together. Moving them closer cut travel time by 18%.
Change made: the client re-slotted the warehouse around these three clusters instead of the old alphabetical layout, cutting picker travel distance by 22% within one relayout cycle.

Churn Prediction

Catching the accounts revenue reports miss

A predictive model flagged high-value accounts that looked healthy on paper but were quietly about to leave.

View analysis
Customer value vs. predicted churn probabilityhover for details
The model flags accounts that look fine on revenue but have high churn risk. At a 0.7 threshold, it captures 74% of churners.
Change made: the client moved its retention team off a manual "recent complaint" list and onto this model's 0.7-threshold queue, which now drives their weekly save-call priority.
